The parts of me that don't fit in a bullet point.

teaching runs deep

Before I wrote software for a living I taught it — as a TA for BYU–Idaho's programming course, where I graded and gave personal feedback on nearly a thousand submissions, and before that at my high school's math tutoring center. Explaining something until it clicks for someone else is still how I learn best, and it's why I ended up as the go-to support person for every system I build.

virginia & scotland

I spent 18 months on a proselyting mission for The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints across Virginia and Scotland. I came home fully independent, comfortable talking with anyone from any background, and self-motivated in a way no classroom ever managed.

analog interfaces

Piano, guitar, and euphonium. I'm an avid reader, a tennis player, a skier, and unapologetic about video games. There's a version of problem-solving in music — patterns, timing, practice until it's muscle memory — that maps surprisingly well onto debugging.

the sawtooths

Every year my family backpacks either the White Clouds or the Sawtooths here in Idaho. A few days of switchbacks with everything you need on your back is the best systems-thinking exercise I know — pack light, plan for failure, enjoy the view.
